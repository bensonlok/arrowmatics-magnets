What are SmCo magnets?

SmCo (samarium cobalt) magnets are rare-earth permanent magnets made mainly from samarium and cobalt. Western catalogs (Arnold RECOMA®-class materials and equivalent SmCo5 / Sm2Co17 grades) position them for duties where NdFeB loses field in heat or needs heavy coatings to survive corrosion. High temperature stability (often ~250–350°C)

Catalog SmCo grades commonly stay useful around ~250–300°C, with many 2:17 families specified toward ~300–350°C (confirm exact max on the grade you buy). That is why SmCo shows up in aerospace actuators, automotive under-hood sensors, turbo / exhaust-adjacent motors, and process equipment where standard NdFeB would demagnetise. Share your continuous and peak temperature on the RFQ.

Excellent corrosion resistance

SmCo has far better inherent corrosion resistance than bare NdFeB. Many industrial SmCo parts run with little or no protective plating; NdFeB usually needs NiCuNi, epoxy or similar. That helps in humid plants, chemical splash zones and outdoor heat cycles — still tell us if you need plating for appearance or galvanic pairing.

SmCo vs NdFeB — which one do I need?

NdFeB wins on remanence / room-temperature pull and usually costs less for the same size. SmCo is weaker in remanence than a comparable NdFeB volume, but wins on temperature stability and corrosion. Choose SmCo when the magnet sees high heat, aggressive environments, or when NdFeB coatings are not enough. Need max strength on a cool fixture? Start with NdFeB N35–N52.

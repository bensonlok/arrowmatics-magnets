02 / Full weld Hygienic construction

Why FULL WELD matters for food safety

Bolted or riveted housings create seams and fastener heads where product and moisture collect. A full-weld plate removes those hiding places.

04 / Inspection & cleaning Ready for your SOP

Plate magnet hygiene checklist

Adapt this to your own SOP. Frequency should follow your hazard analysis and how much ferrous material you find.

  1. Isolate and make safe. Stop the line or divert product. Keep tools, watches and steel items clear of the strong magnet face.
  2. Open or release. Swing the hinged plate open or use the quick-release to lift it out.
  3. Remove and record captured metal. Take off ferrous fragments, note the amount and type, and investigate any change or sudden increase.
  4. Clean the face and seams. Wipe or wash with your approved cleaning method and sanitiser, and confirm nothing remains on the surface.
  5. Inspect the weld seams and face. Look for dents, cracks, pitting, discolouration or damage to the stainless surface. Report any breach of the seal.
  6. Verify performance. Do a cling or pull test with a test piece, or take a Gauss-meter reading on the surface if your programme requires it.
  7. Close, refit and sign off. Return the plate to position, check the latch and mounting, and sign the log.

06 / FAQ Plate magnets, HACCP & ISO 22000

Common questions

Are your plate magnets rated at 10,000 Gauss?

We can build plate magnets with a surface field of up to about 10,000 Gauss. This is a typical rating, not a guarantee for every build. The field at the product depends on the air gap, plate size and how far the product stream sits from the magnet face, so we confirm the right specification with you for your chute, spout or conveyor.

What does FULL WELD mean on a plate magnet?

Full weld means the stainless steel housing is closed with continuous seal welds, so there are no open seams, crevices or gaps where product, moisture or bacteria can lodge, and no exposed screws or bolts on the product-contact face. The weld seams are finished to a smooth, cleanable surface.

Does a full-weld plate magnet help with HACCP?

Yes, it is designed to support your HACCP programme. A sealed, smooth, easy-clean stainless magnet is easier to sanitise and inspect, and it can serve as a ferrous foreign-body control at a point your HACCP team has identified. Your HACCP plan, critical control points and validation remain your own.

Are Arrowmatics or your magnets ISO 22000 certified?

No. We do not claim that Arrowmatics holds ISO 22000 or HACCP certification. ISO 22000 certifies a food safety management system at your site. Our role is to supply fit-for-purpose, hygienic-design plate magnets that support your ISO 22000 and HACCP programme. Customers remain responsible for their own certification.

Which stainless steel do you use: 304, 316 or 316L?

Plate magnet housings can be fabricated in food-grade stainless steel grades 304, 316 and 316L. 316 and 316L generally suit more corrosive or washdown-heavy environments. Tell us your product and sanitiser and we will recommend a grade; please confirm the grade at quotation.

How do I clean and inspect a plate magnet?

Fit a hinged or quick-release design so the plate can be swung open or lifted out, wipe or wash off the captured ferrous material, then inspect the face and weld seams for damage, dents or corrosion, and record the check. Set the cleaning frequency from how much ferrous material you find. See the checklist on this page.

How do we verify the magnet is still performing?

Set a routine in your HACCP or prerequisite programme: visual inspection of the face and welds, a pull or cling test with a test piece, and a Gauss-meter reading on the surface if your site wants a numeric record. Ask us what verification suits your application.
